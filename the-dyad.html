Feel Fully You · a practice from The Touch Reset

The Dyad

A fifteen-minute practice where two people stay with one prompt, so each can say what is really there and know it landed.

15:00
One prompt

The principle underneath it

The speaker is not finished simply because they have spoken. The communication is complete when the listener has understood what was being communicated.

Everything below exists to carry a communication all the way to understanding, and then stop there.

The practice in short

  • Two people, one prompt, fifteen minutes on a timer.
  • One person communicates. The other receives.
  • The listener has three possible responses: Thank you. Clarify that. Summarise that.
  • One turn equals one communication.
  • Roles switch. Return to the same prompt. Repeat until the timer goes.
  • When it ends, let it settle. Do not process it.

Before you start

You are agreeing to a container together before anything vulnerable gets said. Say the agreement out loud.

For the next 15 minutes, our only aim is to understand each other. We are going to stay with one prompt. We will not interject or fix. We do not have to like or agree with what the other person communicates. We agree to stay with the structure for the full 15 minutes.

Then ask the question that makes it real:

Do I have your agreement that you are going to follow the process all the way through?

The setup

Face to face is ideal, sitting or standing, with eye contact when the prompt is given. Remote works too, cameras on where possible, and by phone when that is what you have.

Set a fifteen-minute timer. Both people genuinely protect that time.

How one turn moves

There is a pause built into this. The turn does not begin with talking.

  1. Hear the promptGiven by the listener, with eye contact.
  2. Take it inLet the question actually reach you.
  3. Breathe
  4. Notice what comesWhatever arrives, before you edit it.
  5. Formulate one clear communicationThe one thing you are actually trying to say.
  6. Say it

The three responses

These are the only things the listener may say. Each one solves something different.

I understood you

Thank you.

Nothing else needs to happen. Switch roles.

I am listening, and I do not yet understand

Clarify that.

The speaker adds what was missing. Often that is not more words, only the piece that makes the communication land.

There is too much here for me to understand it

Summarise that.

The speaker takes a breath, finds the bottom line, and gives one clear communication.

Summarise that is the superpower. It asks the speaker to discover what they are actually trying to say rather than unloading everything they are thinking.

Clarify and Summarise are never an opening for another conversation. They carry the communication to understanding, and then you return to Thank you.

The rules that make it safe

One turn equals one communication

One clear communication per turn, then back to the same prompt. Someone can believe you do not understand me while handing their partner five thoughts, three explanations and two grievances in a single turn. The structure distils that until the other person actually can understand them.

Receive, do not extract

The listener is not only staying quiet. They also stop pulling the communication out nonverbally: no leaning in, no excessive nodding, no prompting with the face, no signalling the answer they want.

Do not ride their communication

This does not ban the subject. It means you do not automatically use your next turn to react to what you just heard. If a "yeah, but" rises up, let that reaction go for now, take in the prompt again, breathe, and find a communication that is yours.

If after many rounds, say ten turns, the same thing keeps arising, communicate it. By then it is far less likely to be an automatic rebuttal and much more likely to be what is genuinely there for you.

Stumbling is part of it

Early on, people explain, qualify and pile on context because they are trying so hard to be understood. That is exactly where Clarify that and Summarise that earn their keep.

Why the repetition matters

You are not having a conventional back and forth discussion. You are making enough space for communications to surface that would otherwise stay buried.

The first answers tend to be surface swimming. Stay with the same prompt and another layer appears, then another. I thought I had answered this. Oh. There is more.

How it ends

When the timer goes, the person speaking finishes their one communication. The listener makes sure they have understood it and gets to Thank you. The Dyad is complete.

Then do not process it. Let it settle.

You have added the coffee and the water and stirred everything up. The grounds are floating everywhere. You do not need to do something with them straight away. Let them settle.

Acknowledging the experience is welcome. Wow, that went deep. What does not belong here is when you said that thing about, or defending yourself, or correcting, or challenging what your partner revealed.

Turning the fifteen minutes into a discussion afterwards would undo the safety of the whole structure. If someone knows that whatever they reveal will be cross examined the moment the timer stops, they start censoring themselves. This is the second layer of trust: what I reveal in the Dyad will not be used against me once it ends.

If something stays with you

Later, ask for consent to reopen it.

Something from our Dyad has stayed with me. Are you open to talking about it?

That becomes its own conversation rather than an extension of the Dyad.

Why it works

The container changes the expectation

"We need to talk" puts most bodies on alert. Here the body knows: we are doing this particular practice, there are rules, I know what my partner can and cannot do, and it ends in fifteen minutes. That predictability lets vigilance start to come down.

The usual threats are removed

While one person communicates, the other is not fixing, defending, explaining, arguing or preparing a comeback. They have three possible responses. Over repeated turns the speaker gets to experience: I can say this and simply be received.

One communication at a time creates excavation

Rather than everything at once, one clear communication per turn, then back to the same prompt. That is what lets the layers come.

Being understood lowers the charge

Whatever is difficult has not vanished. But after someone has said what was there and experienced being heard, there is an exhale. The clenching softens, the chest opens, and there is more room around the thing. From there, clarity and possible ways forward become easier to see.

Juliette Karaman
