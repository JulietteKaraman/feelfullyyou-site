01

Why this exists

Something lands. You both feel it. Then one of you speaks, and within a sentence it can be gone.

Was that alright?

Did you like it?

Was I doing it properly?

What did you feel?

Every one of those is kind.

And every one of those can hand the other person a job.

Now, instead of simply noticing what happened for them, they may be thinking about how to answer you. Whether you did it right. Whether you're okay. Whether they need to reassure you.

The experience has become a review.

We tend to think communication means responding.

They say something. We explain. Reassure. Ask another question. Tell them what happened for us. Correct something they misunderstood. Reach over and touch them.

But what happens if your only job is to understand?

  • Not agree.
  • Not fix.
  • Not make it better.

Just let one clear communication land before either of you does anything with it.

That is what these fifteen minutes are for.

02

What this actually is

A clearing.

Fifteen minutes where you both get to say what is actually present for you, and have it received without it immediately becoming a conversation.

You are not there to interject.

  • Not to fix.
  • Not to agree.
  • Not to explain your side.
  • Not even to reach over and hug each other, however much you might want to.

You are there to understand.

One person communicates.→The other receives.→Then you swap.

You keep doing that, back and forth, with the same prompt for the full fifteen minutes.

It is simple.

And the structure is what makes it work.

Because for fifteen minutes, neither of you has to manage where the conversation is going.

You only have to be with what is actually here.

The Dyad, properly

This is the structure underneath everything I teach. I have used it with hundreds of couples and thousands of clients.

The structure is what matters.

  1. 1
    One of you asks the prompt.
  2. 2
    The other takes a breath, lets it land, and gives one clear communication.
    Not a monologue. Not everything you think about it. One thing. Finished. Out loud.
  3. 3
    Then the listener may say only one of three things:
Tap each one
One communication.→Thank you.→Swap.

Then the other person takes the same prompt fresh.

  • You do not answer what your partner just said.
  • You do not continue their thread.
  • You do not explain your side.

You take a breath, let the prompt land for you, and give your one clear communication.

Then: Thank you. Swap.

Back and forth, like this, with the same prompt for the whole fifteen minutes.

You may feel like you've answered it already.

Stay with it.

The first answer might be obvious. The next time the prompt comes back to you, something else may be there.

That is why you don't move on to another question.

Remember: understanding is the aim.

Agreement has nothing to do with it.

You do not have to agree with what your partner says. You do not even have to like it.

You are practising something much simpler:

Can I receive what is true for you without immediately making it about me?

There is no correct answer.

Just what is present for you right now.
