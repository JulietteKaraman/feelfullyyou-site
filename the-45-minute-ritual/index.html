Why this works

Connection needs intention, and it needs structure.

When couples start to drift, the answer isn't always more time together.

You can spend an entire evening on the same sofa and barely connect.

Short instruction video

This ritual works with different parts of connection:

  1. 1
    Understand each other

    The Dyad takes away the usual fixing, interrupting, defending and debating.

    For 15 minutes, there is one prompt and one aim: understand what the other person is communicating.

    You don't have to agree. You don't even have to like what they say. You're simply practising: Can I receive you without making this about me?

  2. 2
    Come back into your body

    We can spend so much time in our heads that we stop noticing what is actually happening in our bodies.

    Touch Base slows everything down. You begin noticing sensation again: warmth, pressure, softness, tension, bracing.

    Because connection isn't only something we think or talk about. There is also what we can actually feel and receive.

  3. 3
    Notice what happens between you

    Before you even touch each other, your body is already responding to closeness.

    The Approach Ritual gives you permission to notice those signals instead of overriding them.

    Come closer. Stop there. Step back. That's better.

    There is no "right" distance. Connection doesn't always mean getting closer. Sometimes it means being able to show someone where you are, and having them listen.

  4. 4
    The Trace Ritual

    This allows one of you to receive while the other leads.

    Short. Non-sexual. Structured.

    Connection doesn't have to mean genital or sexual touch. Sometimes a few minutes of simple touch, with clear edges around it, is enough to experience each other differently.

  5. 5
    Integration

    Finally, you'll come back to the Dyad structure to share what the experience brought up for you.

    This helps to bring the full experience home and let it land.

Before you begin

Put 45 minutes of uninterrupted time in the diary.

Choose a morning or afternoon when you both have some energy. This isn't about squeezing in another thing at the end of an exhausting day.

The sole intention for these 45 minutesWe are here to connect.

Both agree to show up. Put your phones away. Keep one only if you need it for the timer.

Arrive

Stand or sit facing each other.

Take a breath in through your nose and exhale through your mouth together.

Then look at each other. Just notice what happens.

You may want to smile. Laugh it off. Look away. Move back. Minimise the moment.

You may notice that simply standing or sitting opposite each other and looking at one another feels surprisingly intimate.

Whatever you notice, you don't need to change it, explain it or make it mean anything.

Just notice.
Then you're ready to begin.

Why this worksThe structure underneath the card+

The Dyad is a structured communication practice. The card only hands you the prompt. The structure underneath lets you say the real thing and be understood, without it turning into a row.

The aim is to be understood by your partner. You don't have to like what they say. You don't have to agree. Staying inside the structure is what creates the feeling of being truly seen, heard and understood.

A communication is complete when the listener has understood what was being communicated. Everything in this structure exists to carry it that far, then stop there.

Once you reach "Thank you", switch roles and use the same prompt again. Each round goes a little deeper. Staying with one prompt is where the depth happens.

Remember

There is no "correct answer". Just what is present for you right now.

  • No fixing.
  • No defending.
  • No interrupting.
  • No debating.

Don't ride on each other's communication, don't continue the "thread", no matter how much you want to respond or answer what has just come up.

When the timer goes, finish the communication, reach Thank you, then stop.

Don't discuss what was shared. Let it settle.

Part 3 · 2 × 3 minutes

The Approach Ritual

You've practised understanding each other without fixing or interrupting.

You've brought your attention out of your head and back into your body.

Now we bring the two together.

Before we even touch, there can already be something to notice about closeness and distance.

Even when you've been together for years, you may have become so used to being physically close that you've stopped noticing those signals, or learned to override them.

The Approach Ritual is an invitation to notice.

There is no right distance. You're not trying to prove how close you can be.

You're discovering what actually feels right today, in this body, with this person.

The Approach Ritual

I explain the whole practice. Or read it below.

How to do it

  1. 1
    Set a timer for 3 minutes

    Decide who will lead first. Stand facing each other with some distance between you.

  2. 2
    Your partner walks towards you

    The person leading stays where they are to begin with. Your partner slowly starts walking towards you.

  3. 3
    Pay attention

    Somewhere along the way, your body may answer before your mind does.

  • A held breath.
  • A tightening.
  • A tiny pull backwards.
  • A desire to look away.
  • A softening.
  • An impulse to move closer.

Notice it.

When you want your partner to stop, let them know. Say "stop." Put your hand out. Use your eyes. Whatever feels natural.

Then explore. Ask them to take a small step back. What happens? Invite them closer again. What changes? You can move towards them too.

Play with the distance between you and notice how your body responds.

The person approaching

Your job is very simple: listen.

Come closer when invited. Stop when asked. Move back when asked.

Try not to interpret the distance your partner chooses.

More space does not mean less love.

This isn't a test of your relationship or a measure of how close you should want to be.

For these three minutes, let your partner discover their own edges, and show you where they are.

Then switch

When the timer goes, switch roles. Set another timer for 3 minutes. Now the other person leads. They get to discover the distance that feels right for them while their partner follows.

Throughout the practice, you can notice:

  1. What happens when they get closer?
  2. Where do I feel it in my body?
  3. Do I soften or brace?
  4. What changes with eye contact?
  5. Do I naturally lean towards them or pull away?
  6. What happens when I give myself more space?

You don't need to explain any of it yet. Just notice.

Now we touch.

But we're not trying to turn this into sex.

We're not trying to turn each other on.

We're not trying to give the "perfect" touch.

For the next few minutes, we're exploring something much simpler:

Can one of you give, and can the other actually receive?

Part 5 · 15 minutes

Integrate

You've listened to each other.

You've noticed what's happening in your own body.

You've explored what closeness and distance feel like between you.

Before you finish, take fifteen minutes to notice what you're leaving with.

Come back to the Dyad structure you used at the beginning. Choose who will ask first.

Take the prompt in. Let it land. Give one clear communication.

Reach Thank you, then switch. Same prompt. Back and forth until the timer goes.

When the timer ends, finish the communication and reach Thank you.

Then stop.

You don't need to turn what you've discovered into a plan.

You don't need to solve anything.

Let what you've experienced together settle.

Come back to this

Connection isn't something you tick off.

When you notice you've slipped back into logistics, screens, work, kids and co-existing, put another 45 minutes in the diary.

Come back here and run the full ritual again.

You may be surprised by how different your answers, and your bodies, are next time.
