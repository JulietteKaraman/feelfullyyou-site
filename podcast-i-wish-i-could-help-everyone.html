I Wish I Could Help Everyone. Here’s What I Built Instead

The questions people send me are the big ones, and a proper answer needs more than a few typed lines. So I built somewhere for them to go.

Juliette KaramanTouch and Intimacy Expert
A solo teaching from The Scrumptious Woman podcast

My thumb is hovering over the reply button. Somebody has just told me something they have never said out loud to another living person. It is right there on my screen, and I can feel it land in my chest.

I know what a proper answer would take. It would take me understanding the pattern underneath it, the way I do with a person in front of me. Then the phone lights up again. Another message, just as real. Another question.

I wish I could help everyone, and I can’t.

What Lands in My DMs

These are the big questions, the ones people have carried for years. A body that has gone quiet. A partner who stopped reaching. Something that happened years ago, even decades ago, that still gets a vote in the bedroom, or is the reason things do not happen there.

On average, it takes six and a half years in the UK for people in a relationship to ask for help. So by the time a question reaches me, it has usually been carried for a long while.

A lot of you would love me to answer right there in the DMs, for free. I get it. When you find someone you connect with, you want to binge their content and get to know them. If something has been stuck in you for years and you have found someone you want to talk to, of course you want to ask her.

Why a Few Lines on My Phone Is Never Enough

A proper answer to a question like that is what comes up with my VIPs and in my one-to-ones. It needs more than a few lines typed on my phone, or a voice note squeezed in between the rest of my day. So I built somewhere for these questions to go.

What I Built: Scrumptious Confessions

It lives in my app, and it is for women and for men.

  • A confession every Monday, in my voice. What people bring me, what I have noticed as a Touch expert, as a dominatrix, the things I do not always say on Instagram.
  • Your questions, anonymously. You can put one in on any day. I do not see who asks. Nobody else sees who asks. I answer each Thursday, in the app, in my voice. The questions are the gold.
  • Touch Base, with a tracker. A few minutes a week, and you tap your dots so you can see yourself doing the practice. Let’s be honest, we all fall off the wagon and forget.
  • A new prompt every week. One of my Communication and Intimacy Cards, to use for fifteen minutes with the person you love, or as self-inquiry. There is a gong for it and a timer for it, so you do not need anything extra.
  • A library that grows the longer you stay. My answers, my essays, the podcasts, and the answers I have been giving over these last decades. One of my programmes opens for each month you stay.

There is no course to fall behind on, and it sits beside therapy rather than replacing it when something heavy comes up. I am very trauma-informed, and I have worked with a lot of people carrying trauma. That kind of work needs one-to-one support, and there are other places for that. I can help with some of it, and some of it needs more than I can give in an app.

Try This Now: Touch Base

Before I tell you how to join, here is the practice I ask people to do each day. Do it somewhere you feel safe. Your car, the loo, your office.

  1. Hold up one hand. Bring your thumb and forefinger together, slowly.
  2. Take a breath. Focus only on the space between your thumb and your finger. Notice where you might be holding on to something in your body.
  3. Let the top finger land. Become aware of your breath, your heart rate, where your nervous system is, or whether it is coming back online. It can feel like a big exhale.
  4. Say out loud, or think it if you cannot say it where you are: I am safe here. There is no tiger behind you. There is no one waiting to jump you or hurt you.
  5. Now slowly move that top finger so you feel every single ridge. You will probably want to speed up. This can be uncomfortable, so slow down. There may be some gurgling, some burping, your tummy may make noises. That is all perfect.

It brings people out of their busy minds and back into their bodies. People do it before intimacy. I teach it to the person next to me on a plane who is terrified of flying, and they are amazed.

The Question You Have Been Carrying

Keep your fingers where they are and ask yourself the question you have been carrying, the real one. Say it in one sentence. That sentence is the one to bring me in the app. It will not have your name on it.

Two questions, and then I will leave you alone. What would you ask if no one ever knew it was your question? Then picture that question a year from now, still unasked. The same silence. Another twelve months carrying it around on your own.
