Feel Fully You

The Touch Rituals

Twenty touch rituals and seventy-five connection prompts, for two people who still love each other and have stopped reaching. Daily, weekly, monthly, once a year.

All of it yours.

Why this is not optional

Everyone sells you spontaneity. Wait for the mood. Wait for the weekend. Wait until the children are older and the work is quieter and something in you rises on its own.

It does not arrive. A body that has been braced for years does not soften because the diary cleared.

Or they hand you a list. Five ways to reignite the spark. Ten things to try tonight. Always more to do, aimed at two people who are already running on empty, which is why it lasts about a week.

Nothing in here asks you for energy you do not have. Most of it takes under two minutes and happens inside the life you are already living. The longest thing in the guide is one morning a week.

Structure is not the opposite of romance. It is what makes romance survivable for a nervous system that learned to flinch.

Agreement, time, and a clear end point. Your body stops scanning for what this is going to turn into, and only then can it feel what is actually happening.

The order the body changes in

These are the four pillars The Touch Reset is built on, and they only work in this order. Almost everything that failed you before failed because it started at three or four while your body was still stuck at one.

 The moveWhy here
OneRegulate your nervous systemYou cannot want touch if your body still thinks it is under threat. This comes first, before anything else, every time.
TwoRewire your beliefs about touchWhat you were told touch and sex meant, examined and updated, rather than assumed.
ThreeBuild capacity, reconnect to your bodyYour own hand first. Your own Touch Map. You cannot receive what you have not learned to feel.

This guide lives in move one, and puts a toe into move three. Touch Base®, the breath work and the holds are all regulation. The Touch Map is where three begins. That is deliberate. Move one is the piece your body can learn on its own, without anyone watching, and it is the piece everything else waits on.

Before you touch each other

None of the touch in this guide is sexual. Not the hug, not the trace, not the touch dates, none of it. Say that out loud to each other before you start, because a body that is quietly wondering where this is heading is not receiving anything. It is bracing politely.

Three things make touch safe, and they are the same three every time.

Agreement. You ask, and the other person can say no without a reason and without it costing them anything. A no that is expensive to say is not a no, it is a delay.

A time limit. Every practice here has one. Three minutes, sixty seconds, seventy-five minutes. The end is agreed before you begin.

A clear end point. Nothing here leads anywhere else. When the timer stops, it stops.

The time limit is doing more than keeping things tidy. A nervous system builds capacity in doses it can finish. Three minutes your body completes teaches it more than an hour it endures. That is why every practice here is short, and why the ones that grow, grow slowly.

Capacity is built over time. It does not go one, two, three. That is exactly why The Touch Reset runs over twelve weeks rather than a weekend.

The Six-Second Kiss

6 to 20 seconds · before they leave

John Gottman spent forty years studying what actually separates couples who stay connected from couples who quietly drift, and it came down to moments this small: how you say goodbye, and how you say hello again.

  • A real kiss, not a peck aimed at the door.
  • Then stay in it. Let yourself melt into their arms for a few seconds after.

Gottman's own number is at least six seconds, long enough to register as real rather than routine. So longer helps. There is no ceiling to stay under.

The Homecoming

7 to 20 seconds · the moment they are back

The other half of the same practice, and the one couples lose first. Not a shouted hello from another room.

  • Go and find them. Walk to where they are, the way you would if you had missed them, because you did.
  • The same kiss, the same melting into it, that you gave them at the door.
  • Start at seven seconds. Notice what that actually feels like in your body. It will feel long before it feels good, and that is normal.
  • Once seven feels ordinary, stretch it. Ten is where it starts to count. Twenty is where the research says a hug does its full work.

The oxytocin research on hugging backs this up: twenty seconds is where a hug does its full work, ten is a real minimum, five barely counts as contact at all.

These two moments happen anyway, every single day. The only thing that changes is whether you are actually in them.

The Name

10 seconds, or 25 with extra credit · once a day

Their actual name. The one they were given. Not love, not babe, not darling.

  • At any moment in the day, turn to face them.
  • Say their name. Once. Slowly. With your full attention behind it.

Listen along as I do this

A name said with presence says what no endearment can. I see you specifically. Not the role you are playing, not the version of you I have grown used to. You, as you actually are, right now.

Extra credit

Play with how you say it. Surprised. Angry. Playful. Longingly. Hesitantly. The way you would actually say it if you were not thinking about how you should sound.

  • Notice how it feels to receive each version from them.
  • Notice how it felt to say each one yourself. Which did you prefer? Which one did you not like?
  • Bringing awareness to that is the whole point of the extra credit.

The Three Breath Hold

1 minute · whenever one of you is overwhelmed

The instinct is to help. To fix. To find the right thing to say. Sometimes the body only needs to know it is not alone.

  • Reach for their hand. Do not speak.
  • Three slow breaths together. In through the nose, out through the mouth.

That is all. It is presence rather than a solution, and it says: I am staying.

The Landing

90 seconds · before phones, before speaking

Most couples start the day already managing it. Phones, children, logistics, the mental list. Two bodies in the same room, neither of them arrived.

  • Stand or sit close enough to feel each other’s warmth.
  • Look at each other. In through the nose, out through the mouth.
  • Three times, together. Watch what happens between you.
  • ONE SAYS “I’M HERE.” THE OTHER RECEIVES IT AND SAYS “I FEEL YOU.”

Nothing else is required.

Touch Base®

2 minutes · anywhere, and nobody can see you doing it

Your body is always scanning. One loaded moment is enough. A look, a brush, a hand that lingered where it should not have. Your system registered it. You may not remember when. Your body does, and it braces.

Even in loving relationships, that is why touch does not land. Your body is still armoured, just in case.

  • Press your thumb to your forefinger.
  • Move the top finger slowly enough to feel every single ridge.
  • Slower than you think slow is. Like honey. Slower than that.
  • Notice where you want to speed up. Let the thoughts pass.
There is no tiger in the room. I am safe right now.

This is the foundation of everything else here.

The Handshake of Gratitude

2 minutes · before sleep

Each of you names one physical moment from the day where you felt close. Sensory and specific, never “you were kind.” The way they reached for your hand. The moment they turned toward you in the kitchen. The thirty seconds they stopped what they were doing and looked at you.

  • One of you names the moment.
  • The other receives it. No response required. Just received.
  • Switch.
Your beard itched my upper lip when you kissed me goodbye this morning. It felt warm and bristly, and I could still feel it an hour later. It had me thinking of you all day.

Follow along, audio

Specificity teaches the body what to reach for more of. Over time you stop missing each other while you are still in the room.

The 60-Second Hug

1 minute · ask first, every time

Do you have the bandwidth for this?
  • No talking. No phones. No distractions.
  • Look at each other’s eyes as you reach for them.
  • Let your body choose how far to lean in. One breath together.
  • Say it out loud: this is not going to lead anywhere else.

That last part is the whole thing. Safety means the body knows exactly what it signed up for.

The Trace Ritual

2 minutes · the one people find me through, over 2.5 million views

Can I trace your face for two minutes? No agenda. Nothing needs to happen after this.

Fingertips at the temple, or the edge of the jaw. Slower than you think slow is. Watch for where their breath changes, and follow that.

The receiver does nothing but feel. The giver does nothing but give. Almost nobody is taught either one.

What you cannot see in the clip is everything underneath it. No performance, and nowhere for it to go.

The 3-Minute Touch Reset

3 minutes · when you think you are not in the mood

Most people wait until they feel ready. The body does not work that way. You touch, and readiness arrives.

Do you have three minutes? No agenda. Nothing needs to happen after this.
  • Sit facing each other. Both press thumb to forefinger, Touch Base®.
  • Close your eyes. One breath together. Let the day fall away.
  • One of you places a palm gently on the other’s hand. No movement, no agenda.
  • Stay there. Then swap.

The Dyad

15 minutes · one prompt, all the way down

Most couples do not struggle to talk. They struggle to listen without preparing a response, without defending, without it becoming a negotiation.

One of you reads the prompt. The other answers with one honest sentence and stops. The listener may say only three things, and two of them are on the way to the third.

  • “CLARIFY THAT.” KEEP GOING, I HAVE NOT GOT IT YET.
  • “SUMMARISE THAT.” THAT IS GETTING LONG, GIVE ME THE SHAPE OF IT. THIS ONE IS THE SUPERPOWER, FOR BOTH OF YOU.
  • “THANK YOU.” I HAVE YOU. THE DESTINATION, AND THE ONLY THING THAT PASSES THE TURN.

You do not have to like the answer. You only have to get there. Then it comes back to you, same prompt, going deeper, until the timer ends.

Fifteen minutes of being genuinely heard does more than three hours of debate.

Where this comes from

I did not invent this. The Dyad goes back to 1968, to Charles Berner and the Enlightenment Intensives, where people ran a single question for hours at a time, thousands of hours of it, until the structure itself was proven. Later it travelled as pulling withholds, which is the form I learned it in and the form I used with my sister before she died.

What is mine is the packaging. The prompts, the decks, and the way it is taught here.

I have run this with over a thousand clients, and taught it to hundreds of therapists and coaches who now use it in their own rooms. It holds because the structure does the work. Nobody has to be clever at conversation for it to land.

It is a communication structure rather than an icebreaker. You can run it alone to clear your own head, or with a partner, a friend, a parent. The depth comes from the structure, every time.

Taking For Your Pleasure

Weekly · 3 minutes, and only later 5

This one comes from Betty Martin’s Wheel of Consent, and it is the practice most couples have never done in their lives. One of you takes. The other allows the taking. Nobody is being generous.

Decide who is which before you start, and swap another day.

  • Agree the boundaries first. Green is yes. Orange is only if I say so in the moment. Red is an absolute no, and it does not get revisited tonight.
  • Three minutes on a timer. The taker touches for their own pleasure, not to give anything. Hands, forearms, hair, back, shoulders. Never sexual.
  • The one allowing does nothing. No arranging yourself, no making it easier, no performing enjoyment. You are allowing, and that is the whole job.
  • When the timer ends, one sentence each. What that was like.

Most people find taking far harder than giving. Giving is familiar and it can be hidden behind. Taking is visible, and it asks you to want something out loud.

Three minutes is the whole dose. Only when three has gone well a few times, and that can mean a month or six, does it become five. Rushing it teaches your body that touch is something to get through.

Date Morning: The Touch Date

75 minutes · one of you plans it

Not date night, at the end of a day that already took everything. Morning, before the day gets to it.

  • Seventy-five minutes. No phones, no children, no talking about work.
  • One of you sets it up. That person chooses when, and agrees it with the other in advance. The other does not have to think about it at all. That is the gift.
  • Real, structured physical touch. Agreed before you start, with no sex in it, so the pressure comes off and the wanting can come back. Run it on the 3-Minute Touch Reset, or stay longer.
  • Open with fifteen minutes on one prompt, run as a Dyad, before any touch begins.

This is the version where the body is the point. The other Date Morning, the one built around the two lists, is for the weeks the point is somewhere else entirely.

Date Morning: Somewhere Else

75 minutes · one of you plans it

Not date night, at the end of a day that already took everything. Morning, before the day gets to it.

  • Seventy-five minutes. No phones, no children, no talking about work.
  • One of you sets it up. A picnic, a walk you have never taken, breakfast somewhere neither of you has been. The other does not have to think about it at all. That is the gift.
  • Start with fifteen minutes on one prompt, run as a Dyad.

The two lists

Ten things you loved doing as a child, and ten things you loved about the other person when you first got together.

Write them separately, before the morning. Never on the spot.

Say the thing, then say what it gave you. That second half is the whole exercise.

I used to love flying kites, and it gave me such a sense of exhilaration.
I loved how attentive you were and how you would preempt what I needed. It had me feel really loved.
  • Specific enough to picture. Climbing the tree at the end of the road, rather than “being outdoors.”
  • On the morning you read them to each other, one item at a time, alternating. The listener says nothing except thank you.
  • Keep them. Write them again next month and see what moved.

Most couples have not heard what they were like at the beginning said out loud in years. Some of it is still true and neither of you had noticed.

Intimacy, and the way you are touched by life, is structured.

The Breath Bridge

3 minutes · no touch required

Can we do three minutes together? Just breathing. Nothing else.
  • Close enough to feel each other’s warmth. Knees touching or not, let your body choose.
  • In through the nose for four. Hold for two. Out through the mouth for six.
  • Let it become rhythmic. Three minutes.
  • Afterwards, each of you says one word for what you felt.

Is your breath syncing with theirs? Has anything softened in your chest, your jaw, your hands?

The Touch Map: Yes, Maybe, No

15 minutes · always start with yourself

Most couples guess. They reach for what worked once, on someone else, years ago, and they keep reaching that way for a decade.

You offer one touch at a time. The receiver names it out loud, yes, maybe or no, and you write it down.

  • Start with yourself. Your own hand, your own skin, before anyone else has an opinion about it.
  • Then swap. One offers, one receives and names it. No explaining, no justifying, no softening the no.
  • Run each touch twice, the same place two different ways. Once energetic, barely landing. Once firm and certain. The body will pick one.
  • Write it down as you go.

Start with these five

  1. A hand stroking slowly from the wrist to the inner elbow
  2. Fingers through the hair, along the scalp
  3. A flat still palm between the shoulder blades
  4. A thumb tracing slow circles on the back of the hand
  5. A light hold at the nape of the neck
What you sayWhat it means
Yes ✅More of that, and I want you to know it.
Maybe 🔶Depends on the day, the mood, the pressure.
No ❌Not there. Not now. No reason required.

Meh 🔶 is data. A maybe is not a failure. It is information neither of you had, and it is usually the most useful square on the page.

A no ❌ given freely is what makes a yes ✅ mean anything.

The Approach

Monthly · 10 minutes · when the distance has grown without either of you naming it

One of you stands still. The other walks in, slowly.

I am going to walk toward you slowly. You tell me when to stop, when the distance feels right to you. There is no wrong answer.

Somewhere on their way toward you, your body answers before you have decided anything. A tightening. A held breath. A small pull backwards you did not authorise.

That is the moment. Stop them there.

Say it out loud if the words come. Put your hand out. Tell them with your eyes. Use all three at once if that is what it takes, because the point is that they stop, in the place your body chose, rather than the place you thought you should manage.

Then ask them to take a step back. Feel whether that is better.

It usually is, and that is the part that catches people out. You may need far more space than you have ever given yourself. Most of us have never once been asked. So we stand where we are put, we call it fine, and we wonder later why our shoulders live around our ears.

There is no right distance here. You are not being tested on how close you can bear to let them come. You are feeling into what is actually safe for you, today, in this body, in this room.

While they come toward you

  • Does it get more intense when you look into their eyes?
  • Is it easier to look at their chest?
  • Where do you feel it when they come too close? Throat, chest, belly, the backs of the knees?
  • Do you open? Do you brace? Do you lean in, or hold back without noticing you did?

For the one walking in

Do not take this personally. Where they stop you says nothing about how much they love you. This is one person feeling another energetically, before any touch has happened at all, and it is their nervous system answering a question nobody usually asks it out loud.

It changes, too. What felt right last month can be too close today. That is information rather than a verdict.

Then swap, and find out what you never knew about your own edges.

Try Something New

Monthly · an afternoon or an evening

Small novelty, on purpose, once a month. Not a holiday, not a big production. A cooking class. A pottery wheel. A dance lesson where you are both terrible. Whitewater rafting if that is the kind of terrible you want.

The rule is that you learn something together, badly, in front of each other. Being new at something in front of the person who knows you best is its own kind of intimacy, and it is the one long relationships lose first.

This is the small, regular version. Once a year, you do it properly. See Make a Memory.

Make a Memory

Once or twice a year · plan it properly

Couples who have been together a long time stop making new memories. They live on the ones they already have, and those wear thin.

So build one on purpose, at real scale. A holiday. A weekend away, even a short one. The thing one of you has always said you would do and never booked. Skydiving. The night train. The ridiculous one.

This is not the monthly novelty. This is the trip you talk about for years afterwards.

Book it before you leave the room. An experience you talked about is a conversation. An experience with a date on it is a memory.

Where this stops

You now have every ritual I use. Nothing held back, nothing behind a price.

Here is the honest part. These practices work on what happens after you decide to reach. They cannot reach the thing that fires before you decide anything: the half-second where a hand arrives and something in you closes, and you were never consulted.

That is move one holding everything else up. If your body is still bracing, moves two, three and four cannot land on it, and you will conclude you tried and it did not work.

That is not you failing. That is the wrong order.

Moves two, three and four need a room. Beliefs get rewired out loud with someone who can hear what you actually say. Capacity gets built with hands, not paragraphs. Turn-on gets mapped and spoken, and almost nobody does that alone.

That reflex was learned in contact, and it changes in contact. The Touch Reset goes in the order the body and mind actually change.

1Regulate your nervous system. You cannot want touch if your body still thinks it is under threat. This comes first, before anything else, every time.
2Rewire your beliefs about touch. What you were told touch and sex meant, examined and updated, not assumed.
3Build your capacity for pleasure, reconnect to your body. Your own hand first. Your own Touch Map™. You cannot receive what you have not learned to feel.
4Find your way into turn-on. Your Pleasure Language, mapped and spoken out loud. Last, because everything before it determines whether it lands.
