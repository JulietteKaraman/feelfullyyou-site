Long bio

About 230 words

Juliette Karaman-van Schaardenburg is an intimacy and touch expert, speaker, author and podcast host. She is the creator of The Touch Reset, the method that brings intimacy back to bodies and relationships that have gone quiet.

Her path ran from SENCO, teaching autistic children to feel safe in their own skin, to fifteen years with couples and trauma. She speaks openly about sexual trauma, because she lived it: she didn't remember her own for twenty-five years.

At the heart of her teaching is Touch Base®: thumb to forefinger, feel every ridge, "I am safe right now", for two minutes. It is the anchor she teaches first, in every room. She created Rapid Release Rewire Restore® and hosts The Scrumptious Woman® podcast, top 2.5% worldwide.

Her work runs from private mentorship to live retreats, from whispered voice notes to packed keynote rooms, and into organisations. She works with high-profile individuals, therapists, entrepreneurs, leaders and changemakers across the globe, with complete discretion. Known for her outrageous laugh and unfiltered presence, she bridges deep healing and delicious aliveness.

Her full story is on the About page.

An experience

Your audience feels it.

Juliette takes the whole room through it, on their feet and in their bodies, so people leave knowing in their body what is possible.

Move. Cross-body movement. It looks like a bit of dancing. It is bilateral stimulation, the same principle behind EMDR. A whole room drops out of their heads and into their bodies in under two minutes.

Anchor. Touch Base®. Thumb to forefinger, so slowly you feel every ridge, "I am safe right now", held for two minutes. The nervous system learns there is no danger here.

Feel. The shift lands in the body, so it stays with them long after the day.

For organisations

Where you sit matters.

Many people at work carry a history of abuse. In an open-plan office, where they sit changes how safe their body feels and how well they can work.

Juliette shows leaders where to place people, often with their back to the wall, so their nervous system can settle. In her own work with clients and organisations, she has seen it lift productivity by around 30%.
