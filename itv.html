31 Touch Points

£27 · in the app at app.feelfullyyou.com

A touch point is any moment the body registers that it was met. That is a wider thing than it sounds, which is why roughly a third of the month asks for no physical contact at all. None of it is sexual.

The month runs one day at a time. Each day is short, most of them under ten minutes, and they build in a deliberate order.

Length
31 days, plus five short reads at the start and a closing piece at the end. 36 entries in total.
Her voice
20 of the entries carry a recorded guide, so a couple presses play and follows along together rather than reading instructions to each other.
Timing
14 have a built-in timer that keeps the screen awake and chimes softly at the end, so neither person watches a clock.
Images
A photograph on every single entry.
Pacing
The next day opens at whichever comes later: twelve hours after marking the last one done, or the start of the next day. Miss a day and nothing breaks. It waits where you left it.
Device
Any phone browser. It adds to the home screen and opens in one tap. Nothing to download.

The five weeks

  1. Days 1–7Play
    Almost nothing asks anything of your body yet.
  2. Days 8–14Noticing
    You start to feel what is actually happening while it happens.
  3. Days 15–21Giving
    Touch with somewhere to go, and someone deciding where.
  4. Days 22–26Opening
    Slower, longer, and asking out loud for the thing you want.
  5. Days 27–31Staying
    The longest containers in the month.

31 Days Closer

£17 · cards at feelfullyyou.com/my-cards

Thirty one conversation prompts, one a day, fifteen minutes each. The card hands over the prompt. What makes it work is the structure underneath it, a practice called the Dyad, which Juliette has run with hundreds of couples.

Both people stay with the same prompt for the whole timer. One answers. The other replies with only “Thank you.” No discussion, no correcting, no building a case. The aim is to be understood, which is a different thing from being agreed with.

Length
31 prompts, one a day, grouped into themed sections.
Each sitting
One card, fifteen minutes, three answers only. The first answers are the surface. What comes after them is the reason for the timer.
Pacing
One card at a time, the same rhythm as the touch month, so the two run in step.
Device
Opens in a phone browser with no sign-up and no app store. The timer keeps the screen awake and chimes at the end.

Touch reopens the door. Words are how you walk back through it.
