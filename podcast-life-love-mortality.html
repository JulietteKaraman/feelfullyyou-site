What Sitting With Death Taught Me About Living

On being a death doula, staying close to my children's father after our marriage ended, and what facing an ending teaches you about every relationship still open.

Juliette KaramanTouch and Intimacy Expert
A solo teaching from The Scrumptious Woman podcast

Part of my work, outside the touch and intimacy world most people know me for, has been sitting with people as they die. I want to talk about what that role has taught me, because it changes how you treat the relationships that are still very much alive.

Being Seen Is the Whole Thing

In an age where most of our connection happens through a screen, I keep coming back to the same finding, over and over, at every bedside I have sat beside: what people actually need is to be seen, heard, understood, and loved. Not impressed. Not managed. Seen. That is true for someone in their final weeks, and it is equally true for the person across the breakfast table from you tomorrow morning.

Staying Close to My Ex-Husband

My children's father and I are no longer married, and we are still close. We co-parent four children together, and that closeness was a choice, not an accident. It would have been easier, in the short term, to let resentment run the relationship. We chose presence instead, because our children needed both of us intact, and because the connection itself was worth protecting even after the form of it changed.

Preparing Someone for Death

Helping someone prepare to die is one of the greatest honours of my life, and one of the hardest. It asks for total presence, no fixing, no rushing toward a cure that is not coming. What I witness, again and again, is forgiveness arriving late but arriving. Resolutions people wish they had made years earlier. A depth of introspection that most of us only reach for when time runs out.

I do not say this to be morbid. I say it because you do not have to wait for an ending to have that conversation. Slow down now. Say the thing now. Honour the relationship while it is still ordinary and everyday, not just when it becomes urgent.

"Every connection is a note in your life's melody. Treasure it while you are still able to add to the song."
