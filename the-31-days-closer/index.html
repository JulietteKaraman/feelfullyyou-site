What this is

You already know the structure.

Now you need something worth bringing into it.

31 Days Closer gives you thirty-one prompts, in a deliberate order, with one new prompt opening each morning in the app.

So there is nothing to choose.

  • No scrolling through cards deciding which one you both feel like answering.
  • No "that one's too much tonight."
  • No spending the first five minutes negotiating what you're actually going to talk about.

You open the app. Today's prompt is there.

That is the prompt you stay with for your fifteen minutes.

Tomorrow, the next one opens.

You can't race through them, and if life gets in the way, you haven't fallen behind.

One day.·One prompt.·Fifteen minutes.

The structure stays the same.

What changes is where the conversation takes you.

02

The order is doing something

These aren't thirty-one random prompts.

They move through five stretches, and they're in this order for a reason.

Seeing each otherDays 1 to 7

Seven days of noticing each other again, including some of what you may have stopped noticing.

Understanding each otherDays 8 to 14

Seven days of getting underneath what is actually going on for each of you.

Touch and desireDays 15 to 21

Seven days bringing touch, wanting and desire into the conversation.

Honesty and repairDays 22 to 28

Seven days making room for some of what has been difficult to say, after you've had three weeks practising how to listen and be heard within the structure.

Choosing usDays 29 to 31

Three days at the end looking at what you're choosing together and what you're both saying yes to.

The order matters.

You don't begin with the conversations that might ask the most of you.

  • You begin by seeing each other.
  • Then understanding each other.
  • Then you move into touch and desire.
  • Then honesty and repair.
  • And finally, what you're choosing together.

By the time you reach the later prompts, you've spent weeks practising the same thing:

One clear communication.→Receive it.→Thank You.→Swap.

So the questions can deepen while the structure stays familiar.

04

How to run it

You already know the Dyad. This is the same structure, with a new prompt waiting for you each day.

  1. 1
    Choose the 15-minute timer

    In the app, with today's prompt.

  2. 2
    One of you asks today's prompt

    The other takes a breath, lets it land, and gives one clear communication.

    Not a speech. One thing, finished, out loud.

  3. 3
    The listener responds with one of the three answers
Thank youI have understood.
Clarify thatHelp me understand.
Summarise thatGive me the bottom line.

When you reach Thank You, swap. Same prompt. Other person.

Keep going back and forth with the same prompt until the timer goes.

Don't ride on each other's communication. When it's your turn, don't answer what they just said or continue their thread.

Take the prompt fresh and give your own communication.

  • No fixing.
  • No defending.
  • No interrupting.
  • No debating.

Understanding is the aim. Agreement isn't required.

When the timer goes, finish the communication you're in, reach Thank You, and stop.

Let what was said land.

When to do it

Choose a time when you both have some capacity for it. A morning. An afternoon. A weekend.

It may well not be at the end of a long day, when one or both of you are already tired or distracted.

You only need fifteen minutes. Protect those fifteen minutes.

  1. 1
    Phones in another room

    Keep one only for the app and timer.

  2. 2
    Sit or stand facing each other
  3. 3

If a day gets away from you

Don't double up.

Just pick it up again the next day. The card will still be there.

You haven't fallen behind, and you don't need to catch up.

The point isn't to get through thirty-one prompts as quickly as possible.

The point is to stay with one.

One prompt. Fifteen minutes. Then come back for the next one when it opens.

Miss a day? Come back. Miss a week? Come back. You simply continue from where you left off.

If you lose the structure

You probably will at some point. That's okay.

One of you starts defending, explaining or debating.+

Pause. You don't need to work out whose fault it was or how you got there. Just name it:

"We've left the three answers. Let's come back."

Then return to today's prompt and the structure.

One of you gets emotional.+

Take a moment. Emotion doesn't automatically mean you need to stop. It doesn't mean you have to keep going either.

The person speaking gets to decide whether they want to continue or pause.

You don't need to immediately soothe them, pull more out of them or make the feeling go away.

The structure never overrides someone's wish to pause or stop.

It turns into a row.+

Stop the timer. Don't force yourselves through the remaining minutes just because today's card is open.

Leave it there. Come back another day.

Stopping cleanly is part of knowing when the structure is no longer helping.

Your partner doesn't want to do it.+

Do today's prompt on your own. Choose the 10-minute timer in the app.

Read the prompt out loud and answer it out loud to the room, or write your answer down.

When you run dry, read the same prompt again. Same question. Again. And again. Until the timer goes.

You can keep moving through the thirty-one days on your own.
