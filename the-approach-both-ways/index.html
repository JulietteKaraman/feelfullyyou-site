01

Why the second half exists

In When She Stops Reaching, you spent five minutes finding out where she wanted you to stop.

The first half

You moved.

She chose the distance.

You listened.

This half

She moves.

You choose the distance.

You notice.

But that only showed you one direction.

You have edges too.

So now you change places. This time, she approaches. You stay where you are.

And instead of watching for where she wants the distance to stop, you're noticing where you do.

  • You might want her closer.
  • You might want more space.
  • You might change your mind halfway through.
  • You might not know what you want at all.

All of that is information.

This isn't about making the ritual "fair" by giving you both a turn. It's about experiencing both sides of the same practice:

What is it like to listen to someone else's edge?

What is it like to have one of your own?

02

What she is doing in this half

She isn't just walking towards you to give you a turn.

She's giving you the opportunity to show her something she cannot decide for you:

Where do you want her?

  • Not where you think she wants to be.
  • Not where a loving partner should want her.
  • Not how close you're capable of tolerating.

Where do you actually want the distance to be right now?

That's what you're finding out. If your answer is I don't know, that's information too.

The point isn't to manufacture a boundary so she has something to respond to. It isn't to stop her just to prove that you can.

The stop only matters if it's true.

  • If you want her closer, let her closer.
  • If you want her to stop, tell her.
  • If you want more space after you've already invited her in, you can change the distance again.

The practice is not: Can I prove that I have limits?

It's: Can I notice what I want, and let her know?

03

How to run it

Watch us, or listen first
The Approach Ritual

It's the same recording as the first half, and it covers both directions. Or read the instructions below. Either way works.

  1. 1
    Set a timer for five minutes

    Stand facing each other with some distance between you.

  2. 2
    This time, you stay where you are

    She moves towards you. Slowly. A step. Then a pause.

  3. 3
    Your job is to notice

    As she gets closer, pay attention to what is actually happening in you.

  • A held breath.
  • A tightening.
  • A small movement backwards.
  • Wanting to look away.
  • Softening.
  • Wanting her closer.
  • Or nothing obvious at all.

Don't hunt for a signal because you think you're supposed to have one. Just notice what is there.

When you want her to stop, tell her.

Say stop. Put a hand out. Or use whatever clear signal you've both agreed on.

Then explore the distance. You can ask her to take a small step back. Notice what that's like. Invite her closer again if you want to. Notice what changes. Move towards her yourself if you want to.

The distance isn't a test you have to get right. Let it move around for the five minutes.

Your job is to notice what you want, and practise letting that be known.

04

What makes this hard for you specifically

Two things may show up.

One

You may want to be generous.

You may want to let her come as close as she likes. Because stopping her can feel like rejection. Especially if you've spent a long time trying to close the distance between you.

You finally have her walking towards you. Why would you stop her?

Notice that. Because the question isn't whether you can tolerate her being closer. The question is:

Is this actually the distance I want right now?

If it is, let her come closer. If it isn't, let yourself say stop. Not to prove something. Not to make a point. Because it's true.

Two

You may not know.

You might be waiting for some enormous signal that tells you exactly where your edge is. It may not come.

You may feel very little. You may genuinely not know whether you want her closer or further away.

Don't manufacture an answer. Stay with the distance. Notice what you can notice.

And if the truest thing at the end is:

"I couldn't really tell what I wanted."

Say that. Not knowing is still something you noticed.

Why the stop matters

You may want to skip the stopping. Especially if she is moving towards you and part of you has wanted exactly that.

But remember what you're practising. You're not trying to get her as close as possible. You're finding out what happens when the distance is something you can respond to as well.

A genuine stop gives her information. Here is where I want you right now.

When you let her come closer because you genuinely want her closer, that gives her information too. That's the important part.

You're no longer treating every distance as interchangeable. Closer isn't automatically better. Further away isn't automatically rejection.

You're letting the distance be specific. You're letting yourself have a say in it.

The goal isn't to become a man who says no more often. It's not to create limits for the sake of having them.

It's to make your yes a yes, your no a no, and your I-don't-know an I-don't-know.

That's much more useful than automatically giving the answer you think you're supposed to give.

The noticing questionsNot a checklist. Don't answer them out loud during the five minutes.+
  1. What happens as she gets closer?
  2. Where do I notice it?
  3. Do I want her closer, further away, or exactly where she is?
  4. What changes when we make eye contact?
  5. Do I find myself leaning towards her or pulling away?
  6. What happens when I give myself more space?
  7. Do I know what I want right now?

You don't need an answer to all of them. One thing you genuinely notice is enough.

05

Finish the same way

When the five-minute timer goes, stop. Say one true thing about what you noticed.

One sentence. Then stop talking.

For example"I noticed I wanted to let you come all the way in, whether I wanted it or not."
"I noticed I couldn't really tell what I wanted."
"I noticed I wanted you closer than I expected."
"I noticed I wanted more space when you made eye contact with me."
  • No question on the end.
  • No explanation.
  • No fishing for what she noticed.

One true thing. Full Stop.

Then leave it there.

06

Then land it: fifteen minutes

You've just had an experience together that you haven't tried to explain while it was happening. That's deliberate.

During the Approach Ritual, the job was to notice and respond to the distance, not talk about it.

Now you get to put some of what you noticed into words.

You'll use one prompt for the whole fifteen minutes. One of you asks the prompt. The other takes a breath, lets it land, and gives one clear communication. One thing. Finished. Out loud. Not a speech.

The prompt

For the full fifteen minutes, use only this one. Don't add another prompt when you think you've finished this one. Don't start interviewing each other about what happened. Stay with the same question.

Each time it's your turn, let it land again and see what is there now.

A win might be"I stopped you when I actually wanted you to stop."
An insight might be"I realised I was waiting for a huge signal instead of noticing the small one."
A learning might be"I wanted you closer than I thought I would."

Or it might have nothing to do with those examples. Don't go looking for the right insight. Tell the truth about what you noticed.

After

What tends to come up

You may think you have nothing more to say after the first answer. Stay with the prompt. There may be more underneath the obvious thing.

  • What it was like to approach without deciding how close you got.
  • What it was like to be approached and have a say in the distance.
  • Whether saying stop felt easy, difficult or completely unavailable.
  • Whether your stop came quickly or you noticed what you wanted before you actually said it.
  • Whether you changed your mind and wanted them closer after asking for more space, or the other way round.
  • What happened when you made eye contact.
  • Whether you wanted to laugh, move, look away or break the structure.
  • Whether it felt serious, playful, awkward, easy, uncomfortable, or none of those.

Don't work through that list. It isn't another set of questions. It's simply there to show you how many different things can happen inside five minutes of distance.

Whatever you actually noticed is what belongs in the fifteen.

When the timer goes

Finish the communication you're in. Let the listener respond. Reach Thank You. Then stop.

  • Don't squeeze in one more because you were getting somewhere.
  • Don't turn the Dyad into a conversation about everything you just said.
  • Don't immediately start analysing the ritual together.

The structure has an edge too. Five minutes for the Approach Ritual. Fifteen minutes to land it. Then you're done.

Let what happened be enough for today.

What you might notice

The reversed Approach Ritual can show you something the first direction couldn't: what happens when the distance is yours to choose.

  • You may notice that you let her come closer than you actually wanted.
  • Or that you stopped her earlier than you expected.
  • You may want more space and then change your mind.
  • You may discover that saying stop is harder than feeling the stop. Or that saying it is easy.

You may notice you are looking for a dramatic body signal when what you actually have is something much quieter:

Not yet.That's enough.Closer.Back a little.I don't know.

You may also feel very little. None of those responses needs to mean anything bigger about you or your relationship.

This isn't a test of how close you are. The distance you choose today isn't a verdict on how much you love or want each other. It's a reading of this moment.

Can I notice what I want with the distance between us, and let you know?

If you lose the structure

You couldn't bring yourself to stop her.+

She came all the way in and you never said it. Don't turn that into failure. Notice what actually happened.

Maybe you wanted her that close. Maybe you knew you wanted more space and couldn't say it. Maybe you couldn't tell. Those are three different experiences.

At the end, say the one that's true. "I wanted you that close." "I wanted to stop you and I didn't." "I couldn't tell what I wanted." Full Stop.

You stopped her because you thought you were supposed to.+

You don't need to manufacture an edge to do the exercise properly.

If you realise you stopped her before you actually wanted to, say that. "I stopped you because I thought I should have a stop."

That's useful information too.

She experiences the stop as rejection.+

Don't tell her she shouldn't. Don't immediately abandon your stop to make the discomfort disappear.

If there's something true you want to say about your experience, say it once. "I wanted more space just then. It wasn't me asking you to go away."

Then stop. If it needs a larger conversation, have that conversation separately.

You felt nothing at any distance.+

Don't go searching harder. Don't invent a tightening, brace or pull because you think one should be there.

Your Full Stop can simply be: "I couldn't really feel where I wanted you." That's what was true today.

The Dyad turns into the usual conversation.+

Come back to the structure. One prompt. One clear communication. Thank You. Clarify That. Summarise That.

No fixing. No defending. No answering what they just said when the prompt comes back to you.

If you can't stay inside that structure today, stop. You don't have to rescue the fifteen minutes.

Doing it again

You can do the Approach Ritual again. But you don't need to turn it into homework. You don't need to use the distance you found today as your starting point next time.

The distance isn't fixed.

Another day, you may want her closer. Further away. Exactly where she was. Or you may not know.

That's why you don't turn four feet, two feet or six inches into a fact about your relationship. It was simply where you wanted the distance that time.

If you do the ritual again, begin from the beginning. Approach slowly. Notice. Stop. Move closer. Move further away. Change your mind if you change your mind.

The point isn't to find your correct distance. It's to become more able to notice the distance you want now, and let the other person know.
