Why Does the Forbidden Feel Hotter? The Erotic Pull

The push and the pull are the heat. Here is what actually runs underneath it, and the check-in that lets your body stay in the room for it.

Juliette KaramanTouch and Intimacy Expert
A solo teaching from The Scrumptious Woman podcast

I have been reading Jack Morin's The Erotic Mind for a while now, and there is a line in it that I keep coming back to. The same complexity that makes eroticism so fascinating is the very thing that can disrupt it. Anything that inhibits arousal can also heighten it. Any thought, any emotion, any circumstance can turn you on or turn you off, and often the same one does both.

Which is why anger, anxiety and guilt turn up as aphrodisiacs so often. They carry risk. They carry conflict. They also animate the whole scene, and a body that knows underneath that it is safe can handle a surprising amount of that charge.

The Office

Think about going to your lover's office in the middle of the afternoon. Closing the door. Locking it. The blinds not quite fully closed. The walls not quite soundproof. A secretary right outside who could knock at any moment.

That slight nervousness is the polarity. That is the excitement. For someone else, the same scene costs too much: have I been seen, what does this do to my job. My partner is a lawyer and has watched exactly that go wrong for people who got caught. So the push and the pull sit right next to each other, "this is naughty and it is hot" and "here is everything that could go wrong," and for a lot of people that edge is precisely where the heat lives.

The Couple Who Kept Adding More

I had a couple in my practice whose pattern I watch over and over. They are adventurous. They had done the office, later in the afternoon, when the building had emptied out. Then, to get more out of it, they kept adding. The fire jacket. The helmet. Tantra weekends. BDSM clubs. Every possible container for intensity.

Every single time, right at the peak, right at the moment that would have cracked them open, they left their bodies. Dissociated. Watched it happen from somewhere up there, not really aware of what was going on underneath them.

What was missing was never more sensation. It was never harder, never more impact. It was always capacity. That is the Wanderer pattern, where the attention can leave the experience exactly when the experience gets big.

"Polarity without a container isn't hot. It's just flooding, and the system can't take that."

Why Your Body Confuses Fear With Desire

There is a famous piece of research by Dutton and Aron from 1974. Men crossed a wobbling suspension bridge high above a gorge, a real drop underneath them, and met an attractive woman on the other side. Compared with the men who crossed a stable, boring bridge and met the same woman, the ones with the pounding hearts and the shaking hands rated her as far more attractive.

Their bodies were aroused from fear. Their minds, not knowing the difference, read it as desire.

So the erotic pull is that tug of war. I want it. Part of me does not want it. Who is taking charge here, and can I actually surrender into that. I like to demonstrate it by grabbing my own wrist and pulling, while resisting the pull at the same time. That is the thing. It hurts slightly, and it is also interesting.

It is less about what turns you on and more about what conditions let you find the charge at all. Charge is what creates the turn on, rather than the other way round.

The Woman Who Holds Everything

Here is what I am seeing in my clients, over and over, in this exact shape. She carries the whole container of the relationship. The kids, the logistics, the emotional weather, the invisible list. Then he says, let's go out for dinner, where do you want to go.

That is not what she wants in that moment. What she wants is for him to take the reins. To say, I need you ready at half seven, wear this, I have got it all handled. For a great many women that is deeply erotic, because she can put the container down for one evening.

Be aware that she will also judge it. Part of her says you cannot boss me around. Part of her says thank God, I can let go. That is the push and the pull again, the wanting to surrender and the bratty pushing away from it at the same time.

In my years working as a tantrika and as a conscious dominatrix, instruction and praise were the two things that reliably created that release. When someone feels safe enough, a quiet "look down, good girl" does something extremely specific in a body. The shaking starts. Said harshly, the same words land completely differently. Said to someone else entirely, they land flat, a turn off. It depends on how that system is wired, what is not fully allowed in it, and what that does in the nervous system.

Try This Tonight, No Partner Required

Think of one thing you have not let yourself fully want yet. Do not say it out loud yet. Hold it a moment longer.

Close your eyes. Taste it. What would it smell like, what would it sound like. Then, if you are ready, name it out loud, because naming it moves the vibration through the throat and out into the nervous system. If out loud is too much today, say it clearly in your head.

Now check in with your body. Not with your opinion about your body. With your body.

  • Green. Yes. Cruising, going ahead, this is good.
  • Orange. A little curious, a little wary. Pause, or adjust the touch.
  • Red. Halt. Full stop. All play stops right away.

Then rate it, one to ten. How pleasurable was that, and how much were you actually in your body while it happened. Whatever colour came up is information. That is the traffic light system, and it is part of my Touch Map.

The Yes Stroke

With that couple, the one with the fire hats, I took the intensity away and gave them structure instead. We started with three minutes of touch, building toward fifteen. Every touch, both of them, out loud: yes. Yes. Yes. Every time they actually felt it. I call it the yes stroke.

Saying yes each time trains the body to register the sensation while it is happening instead of leaving. Over weeks, the capacity grows, and then the forbidden gets to be hot without the disappearing act.

Underneath all of it sits Touch Base, the two minute anchor you do every day so your body learns it is safe here. Start with Touch Base. End with the prompt: tell me what that brought up for you, tell me one win or one thing you learned. Fifteen minutes of that a week builds a whole structure of communication, because you both always know how long it will last. For all of these rituals, the length of time is the structure. What kind of touch, for how long, then the closing question.

Before Any of It, Agree the Aftercare

We have opened up something with real heat here, the animal body and the push and the pull. The softness of the heart belongs in it too, always.

So make very sure you both know your red, your orange and your green before you begin. Then agree the aftercare. Do you want a hug. Do you want to be left completely alone. Do you want a bath run. This is the part people forget when they build a scene, on the tantra side and the BDSM side both, and it is the part that decides whether you come back down together or drop on your own.

Get the container figured out first. Then play.
