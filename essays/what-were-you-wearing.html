What Were You Wearing

The questions arrive before the comfort does. Every time, in the same order, and most of us can recite them.

By Juliette Karaman · 18 October 2026

I read it at seven in the morning with the kettle on.

Four seconds later I was eighteen.

By now you have all read about the Cornell 7. I am not going to take you through it. You have read it, and she does not need me retelling it.

What I want to tell you is what it did to my body, standing in my own kitchen, in 2026.

Not a memory exactly. My body simply went somewhere it had been before and took the rest of me with it.

I put the phone face down on the counter and I could not have told you, in that moment, what year it was.

I know I am not the only one who did that, because my inbox told me so by lunchtime.

“What were you wearing?”

There it is. First question, every time.

I want you to notice how fast it arrived, and that nobody had to teach it to you. You already knew it was coming, because you have heard it asked of every woman you have ever watched go through this, and most of you have been asked it yourselves.

“Had you been drinking?”

Second.

“Why did you go back to his room?”

Third.

“Why didn’t you go to the police at the time?”

Fourth. This one is my favourite, and I mean that with as much contempt as I can get into a sentence.

It is asked as though the first three questions do not exist. As though a woman who has already worked out that she will be asked what she was wearing cannot possibly have predicted how the rest of it would go.

She did not go because she could already hear the questions. The questions are the reason. They are then used as evidence against her.

“Why now, though?”

Fifth, and the cruellest.

Two years. A lawsuit. A national news cycle. That is what it took for her to be heard at all.

Within one day of a woman being handed that case, the President of the United States said this about her. I am quoting. "Letitia James is a corrupt person. She's totally corrupt, and I can't imagine it. And I feel badly for whoever she's looking at because they won't get a fair shake. Letitia James is a corrupt person."

She is the one who took him to court for fraud, and the finding against him still stands.

Two years for her. One afternoon for the sympathy to travel back to the men.

“And you know this how?”

Because I am one of the ones who spoke up.

I was raped when I was eighteen. In Spain.

Then I did the thing I now know a great many women do, and that almost nobody describes out loud.

I blocked it. Completely. Not buried, not avoided, not something I was choosing to keep quiet about.

Gone.

For years I would have told you, honestly, that I was fine. I believed it. I would have said it to your face.

What I could not explain was why I went so hot and cold with my ex-husband. There was no reason I could find. Nothing to point at, no language for it, and a man beside me who had done nothing wrong and could only read it as being about him.

My body had been keeping an account the entire time. I had no access to the file.

It surfaced years later, in a room, while I was doing something that had nothing to do with any of it.

You can be completely certain you are fine and be carrying it anyway.

That is the part I want the women reading this to have, more than anything else in here. Not because you need to go looking. Because if your body is doing something you cannot explain, you are not making it up, and there is nothing wrong with you for not knowing why.

“So what should we be asking instead?”

There is a list for her and there is no list for him.

Nobody asks what he was wearing. Nobody asks how much he had drunk. Nobody asks why he went back to the room, or why he did not go to the police, or why he has not spoken up now.

Every question in the first list is about her conduct.

Every question that is missing is about his.

You have been handed a list of things to not do, and he was handed nothing at all.

  • Do not walk there.

  • Do not wear that.

  • Do not drink that much.

  • Text me when you are home.

  • Keys between your knuckles.

  • Share your location.

  • Do not take a drink you did not watch being poured.

You have carried that list since you were about twelve, and it has cost you an enormous amount, and not one item on it is addressed to the person who would do the thing.

“Isn’t this just how it is?”

It is how it has been. That is a different sentence.

You have carried that list since you were about twelve.

It never switches off at your own front door.

It is a setting. A low, permanent scan running underneath everything, for decades, and your body cannot tell the difference between a car park and your own bedroom.

So you lie down next to a man you chose, who has never once frightened you, and some part of you is still on duty.

  • You tell me you are not very sexual.

  • You tell me you are tired.

  • You tell me something closed in you after the children and you have no idea why.

You have made a man’s appetite the reason you have no life in your body.

Not because you decided to. Because you were handed a list at twelve and nobody has ever told you that you are allowed to put it down, or shown you what your body does when you finally do.

Somewhere this week there will be another one.

You will read it with the kettle on. You will put the phone face down, carry on with your morning, and something in you will have moved rooms without asking your permission.

You will call it being tired. You will call it a busy week.

They asked her what she was wearing.

Nobody has ever asked you what you are still carrying.
