The Capable Ones

Nobody claps for the version of you that just let it be a mess for thirty seconds. They clap for the one who already fixed it.

By Juliette Karaman · 25 October 2026

What the Body Knows

I was the most capable woman in every room I walked into.

Then I woke up on the half landing in a pool of my own blood.

There were six schools.

Not six children. Seven, across six schools, because that is the shape a blended family makes when you put two households together.

Seven timetables. Seven sets of shoes. A list on the fridge in four colours, because two colours stopped being enough somewhere around month three.

I was GOOD at it. Properly good. Nothing got dropped.

Here is what I did not do, not once, in any of those years. Sit down and feel a single bit of it.

Feeling a thing takes longer than fixing it. Fixing it, you can be finished by 8.15 and out the door.

“You were just busy.”

That is what I put on the form, yes.

Everyone told me I was amazing. Constantly. Capable. On it. The one people ring when something is falling apart, because she will not fall apart with them, she will fix the thing.

I got praised for it over and over, in the specific way this culture praises a woman who never seems to need catching.

Nobody was going to catch me. I had made sure of that.

“So what actually happened?”

I fainted. Then I fainted again.

Nine months of it, in and out of hospital, and not one test could tell me why, because every test was looking for a fault in the body.

The fault was not in the body. The body was the only part of me still telling the truth.

Sticky. Metallic. The carpet darkening under my cheek.

Lying there, I made a decision. No. This is not how I die.

“Alright. So what was the fixing, then?”

An old stress response, promoted.

That phrase is Terry Real’s and I think about it constantly. The response does not go away. It gets a promotion.

It turns into fixing, because fixing is the one version of a stress response the world will actually reward you for.

No one ever said to me, running a household of seven, that my constant motion might be my nervous system in flight, dressed up as leadership.

They told me I was amazing.

I was, in a way. I was also not stopping.

I call this pattern the Operator. I manage the experience.

The experience itself, and never the feeling underneath it.

Something lands. A mood in the house. A form that needs signing. A silence at dinner that means somebody is upset.

Before it has finished landing, you have turned it into a task with a next step.

You are already moving. The moving is the tell.

“Isn’t that just being organised?”

I told myself exactly that for eleven years.

Organised is a woman who runs three calendars and never misses a pickup. I was that too.

Organised is also a woman who cannot remember the last time she let something stay unresolved for an hour without reaching for it. That is the bit that is not organisation.

Both of those women describe themselves as calm. Calm does not have to keep moving to prove it is still there.

(I see it in men too, constantly. The one who cannot be next to his partner’s silence without offering a solution inside the first minute. Same engine, different paint.)

“So I’m supposed to stop managing my life?”

God, no.

Some of what I did in that house genuinely needed doing, and doing it well was never the thing worth examining.

The real question is where the fixing is coming FROM.

Is there fullness in you first, and the fixing spills out of it. Generous. Chosen.

Or is there a feeling you cannot stay with for even thirty seconds, so you convert it into a task before your body has to hold it?

There is a reason this lives in your body and not in your head. Stephen Porges’ work on neuroception describes the nervous system reading danger and safety continuously, underneath conscious thought, long before any decision gets made. A body that learned early it was safer to act than to feel has already chosen, by the time the thought catches up.

Very old. Very intelligent. Still running.

The Fix

Duplication. I was running it for years before anybody handed me a word for it.

Rate the charge, one to ten. Say the number.

Then name the four elements. An image. A thought. A body sensation. An emotion. Any order, whichever turns up first.

Breathe in and out. Then go round again. It is never one pass.

Rate it again. The number has moved.

Three things to take

1. Catch the moving. You are already halfway to the kitchen before you have felt anything. That is your tell, and it is the only one you need.

2. Rate it before you fix it. One number, out loud. It takes two seconds and it stops the conversion dead.

3. Go round more than once. The charge does not come off in one pass and you will quit too early, because quitting early is the same reflex wearing a different coat.

I still fix things. I am still the one you would ring.

I just do not let the reflex choose for me any more.

The thirty seconds before you turn a feeling into a task. You have been skipping it your entire life.

Nobody is coming to catch you. You made sure of that.

So did I, right up until the floor did it instead.
