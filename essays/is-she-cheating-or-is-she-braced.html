Is She Cheating, or Is She Braced?

Withdrawal from touch and interest in someone else run on completely different machinery. Here is how to tell which one you are looking at.

By Juliette Karaman · 8 November 2026

What the Body Knows

He has never once gone through her phone.

He has thought about it nearly every week for eighteen months.

The message came in at eleven forty on a Tuesday night. He had typed it, deleted it, and typed it again. I could tell, because half of the first version was still sitting there above the second one.

“She went up an hour ago. Said goodnight from the doorway. She didn’t come over. She hasn’t come over in about two years and I know that because I’ve counted, which is the part I’m ashamed of. I have to ask you something I can’t ask anyone else. Is she seeing someone?”

Forty-six. Nineteen years in.

The question he is actually asking is whether he is still wanted.

Cheating is simply the only container anyone ever gave him to put that in.

Those are two different questions, and only one of them has an answer you could find on a phone.

“Fine. But IS she cheating?”

Mostly, no.

I have been asked this more times than I could count. A handful of times the answer was the one he feared. Most times it was not, and in nearly every one of those, by the time he reached me he had already spent eighteen months quietly certain.

That eighteen months does more damage than the original silence ever did.

Culture hands a man exactly two explanations for a woman going cold. There is somebody else, or you have failed her in some way you have not identified yet.

Both of those put the cause inside a person. Neither allows for the possibility that her body has a state of its own, arrived at over years, running on machinery that has very little to do with how much she loves you.

“Then why has it got worse every single year?”

Because it runs backwards.

First she stops initiating.

Then she stops responding when you do.

Then the kissing goes, because kissing has become an opening bid.

Then a hand on her back in the kitchen makes her tighten.

Then she says goodnight from the doorway.

Watch that list from the outside and it looks like escalation. A woman deliberately getting further away from you, month by month. It looks, frankly, like somebody making room for somebody else.

It is one thing, arriving earlier and earlier.

Her body generalised. What began as bracing against one specific moment spread backwards to cover everything that reliably preceded it, which is exactly what a nervous system is built to do.

Efficient. Protective. Devastating to live beside when nobody has explained it to you.

“It’s one hand on her waist. How is that a threat?”

It is not the hand. Her body reads the next forty minutes it expects to follow the hand.

Half a kilo of warm hand on a waist is neutral. It has no content. What gives it content is everything that has reliably come after it for several years.

If every hand on her waist has been a question about sex, then in her body there is no such thing as a hand on her waist any more. There is only the question.

What she is declining is forty minutes she has no capacity for at nine forty on a Wednesday. Your hand is simply where the decision gets made, because your hand is where the information arrives.

Stephen Porges called this neuroception. The read that happens below awareness, faster than thinking, underneath anything she could talk herself out of over dinner.

“So she just doesn’t want me.”

Here is the bit that sends good men in exactly the wrong direction.

Rosemary Basson published a model of female sexual response in 2000 that reorganised the field, and most men have still never heard of it.

The older model assumed desire comes first. You want it, so you do it. That is broadly how it runs for a lot of men.

For a great many women it runs the other way round. Desire arrives after arousal, rather than ahead of it.

She gets touched, in a way her body can actually receive, and somewhere inside that the wanting shows up.

Stay with what that means for your house.

If the only touch on offer already arrives pointed at sex, her desire has nothing to respond to. It never gets its ramp. She is being asked to produce on demand, and in advance, a thing her body was built to produce in response.

She cannot. So she stops being touched at all, because being touched has become a test she keeps failing.

You are both starving in the same room, and each of you has decided it is about the other one.

“How would you know any of this?”

I have been the woman in that bed.

I was with a man I loved for a long time and never stopped loving. After the children came, I could not bring myself to it. When we did, it took a great deal to reach me. Afterwards I would cry, and I could not tell him why, because I did not know why.

There was no other man. There was no falling out of love.

I had no explanation to give him, so I gave him nothing, which he understandably read as the worst available option.

My body was running a file from years before him, from a night I had no conscious access to at all, which surfaced much later in a completely different room.

He was standing next to a filing system neither of us could see, reading it as rejection, because rejection was the only word he had been given for it either.

Three things to look at, and one honest sentence

Not a surveillance kit. This is so you can stop running the wrong film.

One. Does she withdraw from touch, or from you? Interest that has genuinely gone elsewhere runs warmer socially and cooler physically. Easier company. Lighter. Further away. A body that has closed goes cooler in both directions at once, and she usually looks guilty about it, because she knows something is missing and cannot name it either.

Two. Does it have a direction? Capacity withdrawal spreads backwards, as above. Redirected desire stays selective. She will still hug you properly at an airport.

Three. What happens in the two seconds after contact? The real tell. A body that has closed tightens, then apologises for tightening. Sometimes out loud. More often with a small placating touch of her own, a hand on your arm on her way past.

Listen to that apology. She is sorry. She does not know what for.

One honest sentence I will not dress up. If she is having an affair, nothing in this essay addresses that. You will know, because the three reads above will point the other way.

The three moves that make it worse

Asking her outright. Defending is a thinking act, and it takes her further out of her body, which is the only place the answer lives. You will also have told her that two years of her silence were read as betrayal.

Withdrawing first. She reads it as confirmation. She will be relieved, then feel terrible about being relieved, and that is a poor floor for the next decade.

Trying harder. It raises the volume of the exact signal her body is braced against. An intensity pattern does not come apart under more intensity.

The Fix

A touch date. Three minutes. Timer where she can see it.

You give. She receives. One way, nothing back. Non-sexual: shoulders, hands, feet, the back of her neck.

She says stop, you stop instantly, no question and no face.

Timer goes, you stop. Even if it is going well.

Three minutes hands her body the one piece of information it has never had. This is all it is. There is nothing after this.

Three minutes for a week. Then five. You build to fifteen over a few weeks, never in one evening. Masters and Johnson built the original of this in the sixties for exactly this reason.

Three things to take

1. Stop looking for evidence and start reading the two seconds after contact. That is where the answer actually lives.

2. Say the ending out loud before you touch her. Her body has been guessing at the ending for years, and guessing costs it everything.

3. Build it in minutes, not evenings. Three to fifteen over weeks. Go straight to twenty and you blow her system wide open.

A man I worked with put it better than I can. “I stopped second-guessing. For the first time in years, I knew what she required.”

Required. That word is his, and it is the right one.

The guessing is what exhausts men, far more than the abstinence ever does.

She is braced. Nobody has ever told her what she is braced against, and she has spent two years apologising for something she cannot name.

Neither could you.

You have been counting the days. She has been counting something too, and neither of you has ever said a number out loud.
