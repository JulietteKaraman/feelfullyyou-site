The Skill You Practise Alone Does Not Transfer to Partner Sex. Why You Can't Orgasm With Your Partner

Somebody measured it. You were handed half an instruction, then blamed for the half you were never given.

Juliette KaramanTouch and Intimacy Expert
A solo teaching from The Scrumptious Woman podcast

You know how to do this on your own. Not theoretically. Actually.

You know the pressure. You know the pace. You know the precise moment to stop changing anything. Your body arrives where it is going, reliably, no notes.

Then he is in the room.

Same body. Same hands, still available. Same everything. Yet you are looking at a spot on the ceiling, mentally running the clock, checking his face, wondering how long this is going to take.

Somewhere in there, privately, you decided something is wrong with you.

"I Think I'm Just Frigid"

"Why can't I orgasm with my partner, but I can on my own?" "I can make myself come, but not with him." I have heard these questions countless times.

Especially in the last few weeks. Speaking at an event. Co-leading a retreat. Four women came up to me and said they could not feel anything in the touch date, the one on the inside of the wrist. "I think I'm just frigid. I can't feel that much."

First of all, take a breath. It is absolutely normal.

I am Juliette, touch expert. I teach The Touch Reset: finding the patterns that close the door to touch, aliveness and connection, and learning how to open it again.

Body Awareness Is Three Skills, and One of Them Does Not Travel

There is a piece of research that came out in December, and perhaps nobody has read it. I am a little geeky at times, I really do like to study these things and find out what is actually happening.

A team at Essex ran a study on a few hundred women, published in brain science. Here is the clever bit, the thing they did differently. Everybody else measures body awareness as one lump. One score. Are you in your body or are you not? They refused to do that. They used a proper scale that breaks body awareness into separate skills, and then they asked which skill does what for the orgasm.

Three answers came back. Three skills, doing three completely different jobs.

One. Noticing. Just registering the sensation. Something is happening in my body, in my skin, I clocked it. Noticing predicted how often women climaxed, both on their own and with a partner.

Two. Body trusting. Trusting what your body, that sacred vessel, reports. You are not overriding it, not auditing it, not deciding whether it is being too dramatic. Body trusting predicted how satisfying the orgasm was. Again, both alone and with a partner.

So you already have something useful here. Noticing gets you there. Trusting makes it worth arriving. Two different muscles, two different jobs, and they get sold to you as one.

Three. Attention regulation. The ability to steer your attention back to your own body when it wanders off. Have I turned the gas off. Oh my goodness, am I hairy down there. Whatever it is. That is the skill everybody tries to train.

It predicted frequency and satisfaction in solo sex only. It did not transfer to partner sex at all.

Listen to that again. The thing you get genuinely good at on your own is the thing that does not come with you when you are partnered.

One honest caveat, because I like the real version of science: this is self reported and measured at one point in time, so it shows a strong association rather than a proven cause. It still lines up exactly with what I watch in the room.

"Noticing gets you there. Trusting makes it worth arriving. The steering stays where you built it."

Half an Instruction

Think about how many times a woman has been told by a magazine, a well meaning friend, a therapist, a coach: go away and get to know your body first. Practise alone. Learn what you like, then bring it back.

She does it. Months. Years. She gets really good at it.

Then it does not work with him, and she concludes that the fault is hers. She was handed half an instruction and then blamed for the half she was never given.

Here is what I am seeing with my clients, in my practice, over and over, in this exact shape. A woman can tell me in her own words what works on her, in detail, without embarrassment. She can articulate it. She can show me. Put another person in the room and she cannot tell you what her own skin is doing. My DMs are full of this, from women and from men trying to understand how to help.

She loves him. The skill simply does not live there yet.

This never comes back through spontaneity. I build you the container, and now I can tell you exactly why that container has to have another person in it. The attention work does not travel on its own. It gets built in the room where you need it, with the person you need it with. This is why I love working with couples.

Try This, Part One: The Noticing

This is the one that travelled, so it is worth three minutes of your life.

Turn one hand over, palm up. Find the inside of your wrist, that soft patch between the heel of your hand and the start of your forearm. Two fingers of the other hand.

Stroke up the inside of your wrist. One question only. Is that a yes? Could you feel it? Not whether it was nice, not whether you ought to have felt it. Is my body saying yes to that?

Do it a few more times. If it is a yes, say it out loud.

Now down the same patch, that same five centimetres or so, the other direction. Is that a yes? Did the sensation increase as you changed direction?

Most of you will have no idea. That is the honest answer, and it is the right one. This is what we are building here. Capacity. Curiosity.

Same spot, lighter now. Almost grazing. As if there were hair on the inside of your wrist and you wanted it to stand up. Up, then down. Have a look. This is your touch map. A, or B?

Now add your nails. Down, then up. Is that a yes? Is there an increase in sensation?

The last one. Look down at your wrist while you stroke up. Watch your own hand doing it. Does watching increase the sensation? Then close your eyes, down and up. Does that change it?

That is the noticing. When I am in the room with people building their touch map, pulling together which parts of their bodies respond to what, this is harder than you would expect. The difficulty means the exercise is working.

Try This, Part Two: The Trusting (This One Needs Another Person)

This is the bit the research actually turns on. I do this in workshops with couples, in my VIP days and in my couples work. It is called offers and requests, and it runs in three rounds, all in one direction before you swap.

Round one. About two minutes. The answer is no to everything. One of you makes offers. "Would you like a cup of tea?" No. "Would you like me to take you out to dinner?" No. "Would you like the window open?" No. Two minutes of nothing but no. Notice where that starts to live in your body, and notice the places where you want to say yes and say no anyway. No is a full sentence.

Round two. Two minutes, same direction. Now the answer is yes. I am asking the person making the offers to keep them easy. "Would you like me to massage your neck?" "Would you like me to play with your hair?" "Would you like me to make you dinner?" Then do not do it. Just receive the yes. Notice what your body does when you say yes to something you may not entirely want. Notice which was easier, the asking or the saying yes.

Round three. The last two minutes. Say yes or no, exactly as you feel it. Nothing gets followed through here, it is an exercise. "Would you like a massage?" Yes. "Would you like to come and see my best friend in Mexico next week?" Make sure you include a few you know they will say no to. Then watch where your body starts to trust what it predicts.

Because you cannot trust a body whose no has never been respected.

So you practise the no in daylight, with your clothes on, through a slightly silly exercise, until it is boring. Until you actually recognise your no, your yes, and your curiosity.

Do it one way now and the other way later. Same sitting if you have the capacity for it, another day if you do not. Close with the question I always close with: "Tell me what came up for you there." Then: what was it like to receive offers and not go through with any of them? What did receiving a no feel like? What did saying a no feel like?

Two Questions Before I Let You Go

One. Where would you actually want this? "Better sex" means nothing. Where. How. Which room. Be specific. An ordinary Tuesday or Thursday.

Two. Picture it a year from now, exactly as the touching is right now. Same ceiling. Same clock watching. Same quiet verdict about yourself, carried for another twelve months, while you get a bit better at it on your own.

The twelve months are the part worth minding.

So do me a favour. Reach out, tell me what is going on for you, because there are several ways in. Much love.
