You're Already Co-Regulating Each Other

What's actually happening between two nervous systems, and the one question to ask yourself before anything else.

It's Not Something Either Of You Switches On

Co-regulation isn't a technique you do to someone, or wait for someone else to start. Two nervous systems in the same room are always exchanging signal, before either person tries to, before a single word is spoken. It's already happening. It has been the whole time.

Which means the real question was never whether he's co-regulating with you. He already is, just by being there. The actual question is which direction it's going, and how aware of it either of you is.

Check In With Yourself First

Before wondering what he needs to do differently, ask the more useful question: what am I actually transmitting right now?

Not what you wish were true. What's actually true, in your body, in this exact moment.

  • How grounded do you feel, right now, on a scale that's honest rather than hopeful?
  • Where's your breath, high and quick, or slow and low?
  • What's your jaw doing?
  • What pace are you moving at?

Whatever the honest answer is, that's what's already entering the room. Not because you're doing something wrong if it's not calm, because that's what's real right now, and it's the one thing in this whole dynamic you actually have direct access to.

His Presence Is Already Working On You Too

In a well known 2006 study (Coan, Schaefer & Davidson, Psychological Science), women facing something frightening showed a measurably calmer stress response in the brain simply from holding the hand of someone they trusted, before anyone said a word, and the effect was strongest in the highest quality relationships. Presence did real, measurable work on another person's nervous system, without that person doing anything active at all.

That's the same mechanism running both directions here. Your regulation, the breath, the steadiness, the Touch Base® practice, isn't wasted on him just because he isn't meeting it the same way back yet. His presence, even quiet, even not actively “participating,” is already part of what your own system is reading and responding to. You're each already inside the other's field. Neither of you opted into that. It's just how two nervous systems in proximity work.

Why Asking Him To “Be More” Can Do The Opposite

When closeness starts to feel like pressure, a nervous system that isn't ready reads that pressure as one more thing to defend against, not as an invitation. This isn't him choosing distance over you. It's closer to a system that hasn't yet decided the room is safe enough to lower its guard.

The very thing that would prove his love to you right now, more presence, more openness, may currently cost more than his system believes it can afford. That's not an excuse for him. It's information about where to actually start.

What Tends To Lower The Guard

Ease without an agenda. Not needing him to be different right now, even quietly. A guarded system can sense when it's being managed toward an outcome.

Real choice. An invitation he can decline without consequence, not a requirement dressed up as one.

Small. One low-stakes step, not the big conversation. A system that survives a small ask learns the cost was lower than it predicted.

For Him, When He's Ready

This part is written so it can be shared directly, or simply read by you to understand his side better. No pressure either way.

Your presence is already part of what she's regulating around, whether you're actively doing anything or not. That's not a performance you're being asked to give. It's just what's already true.

When closeness starts to feel like pressure, that's a nervous system doing exactly what it's built to do: protecting against something that once had a real cost. It's automatic. It isn't a decision you're making about her.

The same question applies to you too: what are you actually transmitting right now? Not to fix anything, just to notice. Your breath, your jaw, your pace, that's information, not a test.

One small, real option, no talking required: sit or lie near her for two minutes while she does her own Touch Base practice. Nothing to say, nothing to get right. Just proximity, and see what your own body does with that.

A Next Step For You

Keep checking in with your own state before looking at his. Keep doing your own regulation practice near him rather than at him, no explanation needed, no waiting for him to join in first. When he's ready for more, the timed container (a real start and end, twenty to thirty minutes, the clock closes it, not the mood) will feel far safer to step into than an open-ended request ever could.
