The Felt Sense

Before you explain a feeling, your body already knows something is happening. This is the vocabulary for that: the words for what is actually going on inside you, before the story arrives to explain it away.

Most of us reach straight for the story. Something happens, and within a second we are already explaining it: what it meant, whose fault it was, what it says about the relationship. The body gets skipped entirely.

The felt sense is what is happening underneath the story. Tight. Warm. Heavy. Open. It is not an emotion yet, it is closer than that, and it is where the truth usually lives first.

Just ask yourself: “What is my body doing right now?” Not what you think you should be feeling. Not the word that makes the best sense of it. Whatever word is actually closest.

How to use it with your partner

Instead of reaching for the story first, start with the body. Before you explain what happened, or what it meant, choose the word that feels closest to what is actually happening inside you, right now.

This is not a technique to get right. It is a pause, long enough to notice, before the explaining starts.

“My chest feels tight.” “My stomach feels knotted.” “There is warmth spreading through my body.” “My breath feels shallow.”

This vocabulary is a foundation of Week 1 in The Touch Reset, alongside Touch Base® and the Dyad. Naming the felt sense first keeps both people in the body, not just in the story, while the rest of the conversation happens.
