Why Can I Feel It With Someone Else, But Not the Man I Love?

Desire comes alive with a stranger and goes quiet with the partner you love. Here is what the body is actually doing, and one stupidly simple exercise that changes it.

Juliette KaramanTouch and Intimacy Expert
A solo teaching from The Scrumptious Woman podcast

A couple came to me in separate bedrooms. She said she wanted more than what she called a wham-bam, thank you, ma'am. He heard criticism and shut down. When I told them it takes about forty minutes for a woman to become engorged enough to be penetrated, it blew his mind, and off camera he said, I do not have time for that. You could see her fold. She said she wanted to feel wanted. He heard rose petals and romance and a bar he could never clear. They had not agreed on much for months. So I stopped sitting them down to talk about feelings and took them to the kitchen instead.

The Tea Exercise

I use this in workshops all over the world. It is stupidly simple, and deceptive. I asked him to make her a cup of tea, and I asked her to be very precise about how she wanted it. Put out a tea station: different cups, silverware, lemon, teas, milks, sweeteners. Then let me demonstrate what precise means.

The blue cup with the saucer with the gold rim. A bag of Bengal spice. Hot water from the kettle. Stir it twice, take the bag out with the spoon, do not squeeze it, that leaves too much tannin, and rest the bag on the saucer in case I want it stronger. He raised his eyebrows, that is a lot. I asked him to repeat it back, and he got it almost right. Then it was her turn. She laughed, got a little embarrassed, went quiet. Go ahead, I said. Ask for the tea exactly how you desire it. Not for what is easy. Exactly how you want it.

The Adjustment

He made it and brought it over. Her eyes lit up, and then I saw it, the tiniest brace in her jaw, a slight tightening in her back. So I had her adjust him. I call it the sandwich. First, thank you for getting the tea in the right cup. Then the change: go back and adjust this one thing. Then, and thank you for doing that for me, I love it when you bring me tea. He huffed and rolled his eyes. If this is how you are about a cup of tea, I said, imagine how hard she finds it to ask you to go a bit slower, a bit to the left.

He brought it back and slammed it down, spilling it. I saw her brace again, so I intervened. Let's do that over. This is the beauty of it. We do not always get it right, and we can simply pause and begin again. He went back, got it just right, and his little boy who wants to please came out. He put it down and looked up, did I get it right? She took the first taste and her whole body expanded, glowing, tears filling her eyes. Yes. This is exactly what I wanted. Thank you.

Every time I run this, the feedback is the same: something this simple was so impactful. Once you know how to ask for what you want and to give adjustments, the awkwardness in the bedroom falls away. When we expect that asking is too much, we do not get the touch we want. We take scraps.

Why a Stranger's Touch Lands

So why can you feel it with a stranger and not with him? A stranger sees you with curiosity and asks nothing of you. That touch is not going to lead to sex, so nothing is at stake. The Pilates instructor holds your foot a moment too long and suddenly your body is alive, because you are not trying to preempt where it goes.

With your partner, touch got loaded. The stroke on the neck, the nuzzle, the kiss on the earlobe learned a path. Your body braces before his hand even arrives. That brace is not rejecting him. It knows the old path and may not be ready for it. It is running on old information.

What You Can Do This Week

Take ten minutes. One of you makes the other a cup of tea, exactly as I described. The other asks first, then receives, then adjusts with the sandwich, until it is just right. Yes, it may feel awkward at first. If you can stay with the awkwardness over a cup of tea, imagine what becomes possible when he touches you and you say, a little to the left. It is all building capacity.

A couple who had not had sex in 23 years came to me. Three months in, with sex off the table first, they were not on a second honeymoon, and they had their best year in business. What is stuck in your touch life always bleeds into everything else. Not fireworks. Touch that lands, safety that builds into the body, and from there you build.

"Your body did not stop wanting him. It stopped feeling open enough and safe enough to really open. That is the pattern, and patterns can change."
