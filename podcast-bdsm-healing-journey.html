What BDSM Taught Me About Healing From Assault

A story I kept veiled for over two decades, and what somatic work through BDSM taught me about reclaiming a body that had been harmed.

Juliette KaramanTouch and Intimacy Expert
A solo teaching from The Scrumptious Woman podcast

Content note: this episode discusses sexual assault, and BDSM as a modality for processing trauma. Read only if you have the capacity for it right now.

There is a part of my story I kept veiled for more than two decades, even from people who were close to me. During my youth in Spain, I was sexually assaulted. I did not have language for it then, and I built a life around not looking directly at it. It stayed dormant, the way these things do, until it did not.

Confronting What I Had Buried

Facing that memory, after so many years, was not a single moment. It was a slow, deliberate act of courage, done in the company of mentors I trusted, using somatic practices, impact play, bondage, held inside consent and structure rather than chaos. What I want to be precise about is this: it was never about pain or pleasure as the point. It was about my body getting to choose, this time, what happened to it.

BDSM as a Container, Not a Costume

People make assumptions about BDSM that miss what it actually offers. Done well, with real consent and real structure, it is one of the most honest containers I know for emotional release. It gave my nervous system a way to complete a response that had been frozen for twenty years, agency, choice, a felt sense that I could say yes or no and be heard either way.

What I See in Others Now

In my own work as a somatic healer, I now witness this same process in other people carrying their own held trauma. The relief is not theatrical. It is quiet, physical, a body finally exhaling something it has carried since long before the person in front of me knew how to name it.

"Healing is a journey, and you are worthy of every step along the way, including the ones that take twenty years to reach."
